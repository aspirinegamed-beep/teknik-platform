Two-factor authentication: why it matters and how to set it up

An extra layer of protection that stops most account takeovers.

Two-factor authentication requires a second proof of identity beyond a password, such as a code from an app or a physical security key. This means that even if a password leaks, an attacker still cannot access the account without that second factor.

Authenticator apps are generally more secure than SMS codes, since text messages can be intercepted through SIM-swapping attacks. Enabling two-factor authentication on email and financial accounts first offers the highest immediate protection.
